# DV skills — intake

Ten questions. Your answers decide which skills get written next, and they matter more than any
amount of research, because the research can tell me what DV engineers do in general and only you can
tell me what *your* team does over and over.

Rough answers are fine. Bullet points are fine. "I don't know, ask X" is a useful answer.

**Please do not paste anything confidential** — no internal paths, host names, customer names, tool
configuration, or spec text. Describe the shape of a thing rather than the thing itself. Everything
here gets written into skills that live outside the firewall until your team fills in the blanks.

---

**1. What does your team do most days that feels like the same work as yesterday?**
The thing where you think "we have done this exact dance a hundred times."

**2. What do you personally re-explain most often?**
The explanation you have given to four different people in the last year. That explanation is a
skill, almost word for word.

**3. Where do new joiners get stuck?**
Specifically: what takes a new engineer days that a five-year engineer does in minutes — and is it
because the knowledge is hard, or just because it is undocumented?

**4. What does a bad week look like?**
Walk me through the last one. What went wrong, what did people spend time on, and which part of that
was avoidable if someone had known something?

**5. Which handoffs go badly?**
Between engineers, between blocks, between DV and design, between your team and someone else's. What
information is missing when work arrives at your team, and what do you get asked for repeatedly when
it leaves?

**6. What is already written down, and does anyone read it?**
Wikis, checklists, onboarding docs, that one spreadsheet. If people don't use it, why not?

**7. When your engineers use an AI assistant today, what do they use it for — and where does it
disappoint them?**
Both halves matter. The disappointment tells me where a skill would help and where it would just add
noise.

**8. What is actually text?**
Which of the artefacts your team works with are readable text files — logs, filelists, reports,
plans, scripts, RTL, waivers — as opposed to binary or locked inside a GUI? An assistant can only
help with the first kind.

**9. What would you never let an assistant touch?**
Anything where a plausible-but-wrong answer is expensive or dangerous. I want to write skills that
refuse to guess in exactly those places.

**10. If one thing on your team's plate got two hours faster every week, what would you pick?**
Free choice. This is the tie-breaker.

## Two things I need to know for the mechanics

**A. Cursor version.** Skills need Cursor 2.4 or newer (it added Agent Skills in January 2026).
In Cursor, Help → About. If your team is on something older, the same content ships as rules instead —
it just changes the install step.

**B. Where would people look?** The team's SharePoint site, a Teams channel, a repo, or somewhere
else? Whichever place they already open every day beats the place that is tidiest.

---

## What happens with your answers

Six skills exist or are being written. Your answers decide the next set, and they will also tell me
which of the six are wrong. The first one — finding the true first error in a simulation log and
turning it into a shareable repro block — is ready for you to try.

The honest test is small: fill in its blanks (about thirty minutes, no engineer needed), give it to
two engineers, and see whether either of them would still have it installed a week later. If neither
would, the approach is wrong and more skills will not fix it. Better to learn that from one skill
than from forty.
